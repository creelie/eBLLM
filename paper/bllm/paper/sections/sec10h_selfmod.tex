\section{Learning from failure}\label{sec:selfmod}

A repair of an engine usually begins as a sentence in a log written by hand: the builder stalls here, the filter eats the budget there, so the next version should do something else. Such repairs built most of the parts of Section~\ref{sec:brain}, but an engine cannot read prose. Anaxia keeps them as data. An attempt that fails leaves a record that names what failed, changes one rule of the engine, and is closed only when the next attempt, run under the changed rule, has an outcome of its own. This section describes the records, proves that the loop which writes them always stops, and reads the log of the runs so far.

\subsection{Records and the rule book}\label{ssec:records}

A \emph{rule} is a named setting that a run reads: how the object is written down, which filters are hard, how candidates are generated, which stored results may be used as lemmas, what is searched before an attack, what the budget is, and which modes of work exist. The \emph{rule book} is the current value of every rule, with a version number that rises by one at each change. A \emph{change} names one rule, its kind (one of nine: mode, representation, strategy, library, retrieval, scope, reduction, check, budget), its old value, its new value, and the measurement it is meant to improve.\footnote{A change without that measurement is rejected by the package, and so is a change whose stated old value is not the current one. The first rule makes every change testable: the next attempt either moves the named number or it does not. The second makes the rule book a sequence of edits that can be replayed from version~0.}

An attempt ends in one of four outcomes: \emph{closed}, when the target is settled and the result has passed every check; \emph{partial}, when a weaker statement or a piece of the target has passed; \emph{failed}, when the attempt ran and produced nothing that passed; and \emph{stopped}, when no checkable form of the next step exists under the current rules. A record is written for every attempt. It holds the target, the number of the attempt, the version of the rule book, the outcome, the part of the engine that failed together with the measured evidence, a diagnosis, the change, and the outcome of the retry. The diagnosis is one of nine failure kinds, each the answer to one question: was there no mode that could even state the object, did a hard filter take the budget, did the generator produce only failures, could no rule fire, was the argument found but impossible to arrange as required, was the target already settled, was the statement aimed at too strong, was the computation too large, or was the object searched in a form that hides the property?

\subsection{The loop and why it stops}\label{ssec:loop}

The loop is short (Fig.~\ref{fig:modloop}(a)). It makes an attempt under the current rule book. If the attempt closes, the record is written and the loop ends. Otherwise the record is diagnosed, the playbook ranks the nine kinds of rule for that diagnosis, a proposer turns the best-ranked kind that still has a move into one concrete change, the change is applied, and the record is written with it. The next attempt runs under the new rule book, and its outcome is entered as the retry of the record before it. Two guards keep the loop from going round in circles: a change must name the measurement it is meant to improve, and a change already made on the same target, meaning the same rule set to the same value, is refused.

The second guard is enough to make the loop finite, and the bound depends only on what the playbook can propose.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:loopstops}
Let $C$ be the finite set of changes that the proposer can return for a target. Then the loop makes at most $|C|$ changes and at most $|C|+1$ attempts on that target.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Every change the loop applies to the target lies in $C$, and no two of them are equal, because a change equal to an earlier one is refused. The applied changes therefore form a list without repetitions whose entries all lie in $C$, and such a list has at most $|C|$ entries: by induction on its length, removing its first entry $c$ from both the list and $C$ leaves a list without repetitions inside $C\setminus\{c\}$, which has one element fewer. Each attempt after the first follows exactly one applied change, since an attempt that is not followed by a change ends the loop, so there are at most $|C|+1$ attempts.
\end{proof}

The proof is formalised in the module \lean{EBLLM.SelfMod} of the engine's Lean library, as the theorems \lean{loop\_changes\_le} and \lean{loop\_attempts\_le}; both depend only on the standard axioms (Appendix~\ref{app:checks}).\footnote{The same counting lemma gives the bound on descents used in Section~\ref{sec:seed}: a strictly monotone sequence of integers inside an interval of length $\ell$ has at most $\ell+1$ terms, so a descent inside it has at most $\ell$ steps. In the package a hard cap of twelve attempts per target sits on top of Proposition~\ref{prop:loopstops}; it has never been reached.}

The ranking is where the log feeds back into the loop. For a diagnosis $d$, the table of the package lists the kinds of rule that usually help, best first, and gives the kind in position $i$ the prior weight $p=1/(2+i)$; a kind not listed gets $p=1/10$. If changes of kind $k$ have been made $t$ times after a failure of kind $d$, and $w$ of their retries closed or advanced the target, the kind is scored by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:playbook}
s(d,k)=\frac{w+2p}{t+2}.
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:playbook}
The score~\eqref{eq:playbook} is the posterior mean of the success rate of changes of kind $k$ after failures of kind $d$, under the prior $\mathrm{Beta}(2p,\,2-2p)$ and $w$ successes in $t$ independent tries. In particular the prior counts as two tries, its weight in the score is $2/(t+2)$, and as $t\to\infty$ with $w/t\to q$ the score tends to $q$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
A $\mathrm{Beta}(a,b)$ prior and $w$ successes in $t$ Bernoulli tries give the posterior $\mathrm{Beta}(a+w,\,b+t-w)$, whose mean is $(a+w)/(a+b+t)$. With $a=2p$ and $b=2-2p$ this is $(w+2p)/(t+2)$. Writing it as $\tfrac{2}{t+2}\,p+\tfrac{t}{t+2}\,\tfrac{w}{t}$ shows the weight of the prior and the limit.
\end{proof}

So a fresh engine follows the table, and an engine with a long log follows its own record. A kind that keeps failing on a diagnosis falls below the next one after a few tries: two failed tries take a first choice from $1/2$ to $1/4$, below the untried second choice at $1/3$.

\subsection{What the log shows}\label{ssec:modlogread}

The log bundled with the package holds \modAttempts{} attempts on \modTargets{} targets and \modChanges{} changes of rule, in \modRuns{} runs (Tables~\ref{tab:modlog} and~\ref{tab:modlogother}, Fig.~\ref{fig:modladder}). Three runs are the engine's own, and the other \modOtherRuns{} were logged by the attacks that used it between 19 and 21 October.\footnote{The records of the construction run, of the seed run (Section~\ref{ssec:seedrun}) and of the Hodge run (Section~\ref{sec:hodgeround}) were taken from the logs of the runs of 19 October, which give for each attempt its measured evidence and the change that followed, and were written in the format of the loop. The records of the other runs were sent by the attacks themselves, each with its evidence and the rule it changed, and were entered in the same format. The loop of Section~\ref{ssec:loop} produces the same records itself for every new run.} \modBuilt{} of the changes were built and tried; the retries of \modRetryClosed{} of them closed their target, and \modRetryPartial{} more moved it forward without closing it. In the engine's own runs the commonest kinds of change were changes of representation and of strategy (\modOwnKindRepresentation{} and \modOwnKindStrategy{}), the strategies mostly in the search for the sharp seed constant: many failures came from searching for an object in a form in which the property it needed was invisible, and the cure was to write the candidates differently, so that a filter held by construction instead of being tested afterwards. Over the whole log there are \modKindRepresentation{} changes of representation, \modKindStrategy{} of strategy and \modKindCheck{} checks, and \modOtherKindCheck{} of the checks come from the other attacks, which failed differently.

The first target shows the whole loop at work. The task was to rediscover the 15-vertex gadget of Steinberg's counterexample (Section~\ref{ssec:steinberg}). The packaged engine of the time could not even write down a graph, so the first change added a mode of work: construction over graphs, with an exists-forall search. That mode generated planar frames and tested planarity afterwards, and the planarity test took almost every cut, so the second change wrote the candidates as frames glued on one face, planar by construction. The frames were then planar but none forced the colouring, which is a generator failure, and the third change replaced random frames by the goal-directed builder that grows the forcing argument together with the graph. The fourth attempt closed: it found Steinberg's gadget from the rules alone. The second gadget, with all contact distances at least four, was out of reach because no rule could fire from contacts that far apart; one change of library kind, turning every stored gadget into a lemma rule, closed it on the next attempt.

The failures that remain open are as informative. On Erd\H{o}s's question with six colours the engine reduced the target to one gadget, compressed every proved gadget to a single node to cope with the size, and moved to face-aware moves, and each retry failed in the same place: the forcing argument was found, but it could not be laid out with the contacts on one face. The diagnosis became \emph{arrangement} twice in a row, and the change it now calls for, a search over planar arrangements of the forcing argument, is recorded and not yet built. The Bordeaux conjecture followed the same path to the same change. The dijoin conjecture of Chudnovsky, Edwards, Kim, Scott and Seymour \cite{CEKSS16} stopped at once for a different reason: it had already been proved, by a theorem on strong orientations whose paper says that it implies the conjecture \cite{ADN25}, and the first retrieval had missed it.\footnote{The change that followed widened retrieval to the papers after the date of a source that say they imply the target, so that a target settled in this way is caught before an attack starts. The retry found the proof and stopped, as it should.} The loop did not attack it.

On the Hodge conjecture the records point the other way, at a reduction, a lemma and a check. The configurations of four line bundles on $E_{0}^{6}$ had been judged by the dimensions of their Ext blocks, a form in which it cannot be seen whether a piece lies on a closed chain at all; the diagnosis was \emph{representation}, and the change of reduction asks that question first and compares the parity of the shifts the chains reach with the parity the Chern character forces. The retry decided every convolution with at most five line bundles and stopped at six, where no rule fired; the diagnosis there was a silent rule set, and the change added a lemma to the library: a step where the cochain vanishes, next to a nonzero step that is the only way between two line bundles, carries no deformation. With it and a search over the arrangements of shifts the next retry decided everything up to seven line bundles and stopped at eight, where the products along the chains, not their shape, decide. The diagnosis there was \emph{representation} again, and the change read the obstruction through the semiregularity map with supports instead of through products; the retry decided every object supported on the Weil threefolds, at every discriminant, and left the eight-bundle arrangements of full support (Section~\ref{sec:hodgeround}). The chain for $\sqrt{10}$ was admitted as a candidate although a rank count kills it; the check that would have rejected it at admission is recorded and not yet built.

The other attacks failed mostly by aiming too high (Table~\ref{tab:modlogother}): \modOtherOverclaim{} of their \modOtherChanges{} changes answered an \emph{overclaim}, a bound, a lemma or a constant stronger than what had been shown, and \modOtherChecks{} of them added a check that rejects such a statement before it is used. On the Riemann hypothesis the first check rejects any bound on the Liouville correlations below the size that random signs give; the Uniform Collar Bound of one route is smaller than the true correlations by a factor of $8498$ at $X=4\cdot10^{6}$, $H=X^{0.8}$. On the proportion of zeros that are simple and on the critical line the run then learnt three checks in a row: higher-order correlation data count only where off-line zeros are provably rare, a relaxation is read only after it reproduces a proved theorem, and a numerical minimum is a constant only once a rigorous branch and bound has failed to beat it. The change that first moved the target was a different majorant for the large sieve, the Fej\'er kernel fitted to the weight, which is diagonal on separated points and needs no tail bound; with it a window of eight zeros, certified by a branch and bound of about $10^{10}$ nodes, showed that more than $67.311\%$ of the zeros are simple and on the line. The next change gave each pair term of the same window its own weight, optimised by a linear program under the same constraints and rounded to rationals, and a branch and bound of about $2\cdot10^{10}$ nodes certifies more than $67.3198\%$ \cite{BBmh}.\footnote{The previous bound was $67.25\%$, by Alp\"oge and Furman, whose framework the certificate uses. Before a window is certified the run computes its chain limit, which caps every weighting of that length: $0.67322$ for windows of eight zeros, $0.67326$ for nine and $0.67357$ for the whole chain; a sum cut at a few hundred neighbours reads the last cap too low by about $5\cdot10^{-5}$. At the same window uniform weights give $0.6731165$. The window of nine zeros is the change now running.} On the Erd\H{o}s--Straus equation the checks reject a covering argument that ends by choosing a suitable modulus, since identity families cannot cover Mordell's square classes, and two lemmas that fail on small cases; typed crossing then refuted $121$ of $128$ composites on data before any proof was attempted, and the change that keeps each refuting witness with its composite turned one refutation into a remark of the paper that proves the first explicit exponent for the whole exceptional set. When the classes of the three corners of the triangle of factorisations proved to give nothing better, a change of library added the strips of the centre of the triangle, chosen by the engine among $37$ constructions, with a divisor found in the range of Maynard's theorem on primes in progressions to large moduli \cite{May25}, and the exponent rose from $0.6608$ to at least $0.6657$, and numerically to $0.6792$ \cite{BBes}.\footnote{The exceptional set up to $N$ has at most $N\exp(-(c-\varepsilon)(\log N)^{2/3})$ members for every $c<c_{1}$, where $c_{1}\ge0.6657102497$ in closed form and $c_{1}=0.6792$ numerically; the corners alone give $c_{0}=0.6607886973$ and no more, and the method stops at $0.6797$. For the primes alone Dahan's sieve gives the exponent $0.065$, and $0.0943$ with the constant $9/(2\pi^{2})$ of its sieve sum, found by Z.~Liu \cite{Liu26}. The check on constants given by series over primes, the second row of the run, comes from that sieve sum: its constant term, computed to a cutoff of $10^{6}$, was off by $1.7\cdot10^{-6}$ until the tail was estimated.} The union-closed run learnt to look up the ceiling of a method before searching past it, and reached Moffat's entropy ceiling $C^{*}=0.38288\ldots$ exactly, by tangency, positivity of the Hessian and an interval cover \cite{BBuc}. It then took the boundary case of a smallest counterexample with $4m-1$ sets, whose exclusion at $m=13$ would prove the conjecture for every family of at most $52$ sets, against $50$ known. A second extremal parameter, the fewest elements after the fewest sets, closed one sub-case by deleting an element that is a near-twin of a top element; a change of representation, to two intersection-closed families of complements, closed the cases with no domination and with domination only by the two tops of a partner pair; and a reduction to one SAT relaxation over all domination orders, whose proofs are checked by \texttt{drat-trim}, is unsatisfiable up to $m=11$, with $m=13$ running. The lonely-runner run added a shift lemma for powers of two to its library when the kill of Allikvere's pipeline had nothing to act on at sixteen runners, and then a check, after the rows that seemed to persist at four small primes all died at lift levels $64$ or $128$: survivors at a depth cap are no obstruction when the property is monotone in depth, so the search reruns deeper and every result names its depth. The prime gates at $23$, $29$, $31$, $179$ and $239$ are closed. The strings run replaced a symmetry count, which only bounds the number of Yukawa couplings that do not vanish, by an exact evaluation on all $3876$ monomials of degree $15$ \cite{BBstr}, and moved the search for three generations from the quintic to the weighted pencils of degrees $6$, $8$ and $10$, where Riemann--Roch integrality again excludes three families. The Yang--Mills run narrowed its target at once, since each piece of the mass gap beyond strong coupling ends in the Clay problem itself, to explicit couplings with a proved gap in the forest gauge, and moved the range for four-dimensional $\mathrm{SU}(3)$ from $\beta_{W}<0.366$ to $\beta_{W}\le\frac{45}{64}$ by certifying the one-link estimate box by box in ball arithmetic instead of bounding it by hand; one link at a time stops near $0.715$, and blocks of several links are the change being built. The happy-ending run used the engine only to check the status of its problem and logged searches of its own: a graded score, which measures how near each bad subset is to being broken, and repair moves, which push one of its points just inside a simplex of the others, found $11$ integer points in $\mathbb{R}^{4}$ and $15$ in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ with no $8$ in convex position, so $g^{(4)}(8)\ge12$ and $g^{(3)}(8)\ge16$, with certificates checked by the Lean kernel. The next sizes stall at three or four bad subsets for every seed, and the change they call for is a new representation of the start. None of these runs settles the conjecture it started from, and the log says so: the Riemann hypothesis and the Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture end in a stop, the union-closed sets conjecture, the lonely runner conjecture and the mass gap are partial, the last only in the gauge-fixed ranges to which it was narrowed, and the Geometric Selection Conjecture ends in three failures.

\subsection{Credit for the whole run}\label{ssec:skills}

The playbook judges a change by the attempt that follows it, and on the first target of the log that judgement is wrong twice. The new mode and the planar frames were each followed by a failed attempt, so the playbook counts both as failures, yet the attempt that closed ran with both in place: without the mode there was no graph to build, and the goal-directed builder grew its gadget on the planar frames. A change can be needed without being enough, and only the run as a whole shows it.

Anaxia therefore also reads the log as a hypergraph, as HyperSkill keeps the experience of an agent, with one hyperedge for each trajectory \cite{XYH26}. Here a trajectory is the list of records of one target in one run. Its hyperedge binds together the diagnoses met along the way, the changes made, each with the diagnosis it answered, the best outcome reached and the evidence of the last record. The changes of a trajectory that closed or advanced are \emph{strategies}; the changes of a trajectory that ran to its end and only failed are \emph{patterns to avoid}. A trajectory whose last change has not yet been tried counts for neither, and neither does one that stopped, because a stop says that no checkable step exists, which is a verdict on the target and not on the changes.\footnote{The dijoin conjecture is the case in point: its retrieval change found that the conjecture had already been proved, and the run stopped, as it should. Counting that change as a pattern to avoid would teach the engine to stop looking for proofs that exist.}

For a diagnosis $d$ on a target of a run $R$ the memory retrieves along two paths, the trajectories that met $d$ and the trajectories of $R$, and orders the kinds of rule by four keys in turn: whether the pair $(d,k)$ is pruned; the count $\kappa(d,k)$, the number of retrieved strategies minus the number of retrieved patterns that changed a rule of kind $k$ in answer to $d$; the number of strategies of $R$ that changed a rule of kind $k$; and the score~\eqref{eq:playbook}, which now only breaks ties. The pair $(d,k)$ is \emph{pruned} once it has been used in at least three finished trajectories and fewer than a fifth of them closed or advanced. A pruned kind moves to the end of the order, and an exact change, the same rule set to the same value, that meets the same condition is refused.\footnote{The thresholds, three uses and one fifth, are those of HyperSkill, and so is the count $\kappa$, which there ranks a skill by the number of retrieved trajectories that contain it. The rest of HyperSkill is not taken. It embeds the text of its skills, retrieves by similarity and merges skills that lie close together; the nodes here are exact changes of rule, so equal ones are already a single node and nothing needs merging. Its code is not used either, since its repository names the Apache licence but carries no licence file.} The memory only reorders the kinds and refuses changes, so the changes it lets through still form a list without repetitions inside the set $C$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:loopstops}, and the bound there holds unchanged. It is computed from the records alone, so it needs no store of its own and cannot drift from the log.

On the bundled log the memory finds twenty-five trajectories: seventeen strategies, four open (Erd\H{o}s's question with six colours, the Bordeaux conjecture and the chain for $\sqrt{10}$, whose last changes are not yet built, and the next lower bounds of the happy-ending run, whose change has not finished), three stopped (the dijoin conjecture, the Riemann hypothesis and the Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture) and one pattern to avoid. It credits eight changes that the playbook counts as failures: the mode and the planar frames of Steinberg's gadget, the first change of the seed run, which found the next polytope as a cut by a half-space instead of by deleting a vertex (Section~\ref{ssec:seedrun}), the three checks that preceded the Fej\'er majorant in the run on simple zeros, the look-up of the entropy ceiling in the union-closed run, and the graded score of the happy-ending run. The pattern is the Geometric Selection Conjecture: its reduction to one viable vacuum fixed by mirror symmetry is an equivalence, but it and the move to weighted pencils led only to failures. Nothing is pruned yet. The memory now also changes a choice, for three diagnoses. After an overclaim it ranks a check first, where the playbook, which sees that the next attempt after each of these checks failed, ranks a change of scope first; after a failure of scale it ranks a change of budget third, from the certifications of the seed and union-closed runs, where the playbook ranks representation; and after a failure of representation it ranks a check before a reduction, from the runs on simple zeros and on Yang--Mills.\footnote{For the other six diagnoses the first three kinds in the order of the memory are those of the playbook. The first kind to be pruned will be one that keeps leading nowhere after being tried three times; the strategy and reduction changes of the Geometric Selection Conjecture have been tried once each.}

\subsection{Screens and two gates}\label{ssec:screens}

Some failures leave behind a rule that is better kept as a test than as a change of the rule book. A \emph{screen} tests a claimed step on cases where the truth can be computed exactly, and rejects the step at the first case that contradicts it. Passing a screen proves nothing; failing one refutes the step, and the failing case is kept with the screen as its counterexample. Table~\ref{tab:screens} lists the screens and the two gates of this subsection.\footnote{The screens are the module \texttt{screens} of the memory part and the gates are the module \texttt{reformulate} of construction mode. \emph{Correlations of the Liouville function.} Let $\lambda(n)=(-1)^{\Omega(n)}$, where $\Omega(n)$ counts the prime factors of $n$ with multiplicity, and $C_{h}(X)=\sum_{n\le X-h}\lambda(n)\lambda(n+h)$ for integers $1\le h<X$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:randomsigns}
Let $\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2},\dots$ be independent signs, each $\pm1$ with probability $\frac12$, and $S_{h}=\sum_{n\le X-h}\varepsilon_{n}\varepsilon_{n+h}$. Then $\mathbb{E}\,S_{h}^{2}=\lfloor X\rfloor-h$, and for integers $1\le H<X/2$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:randommean}
\frac1H\sum_{H<h\le2H}\mathbb{E}\,S_{h}^{2}=\lfloor X\rfloor-\frac{3H+1}{2}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expanding the square,
\[
\mathbb{E}\,S_{h}^{2}=\sum_{m,n\le X-h}\mathbb{E}\,\varepsilon_{m}\varepsilon_{m+h}\varepsilon_{n}\varepsilon_{n+h}.
\]
A product of independent signs has expectation $1$ when every index occurs an even number of times and $0$ otherwise. For $m=n$ the product is $\varepsilon_{n}^{2}\varepsilon_{n+h}^{2}=1$. For $m<n$ the smallest index $m$ occurs once unless $m\in\{n,n+h\}$, which is impossible, and for $m>n$ the index $n$ occurs once. So only the $\lfloor X\rfloor-h$ diagonal terms remain. Averaging $\lfloor X\rfloor-h$ over $h=H+1,\dots,2H$ gives $\lfloor X\rfloor-\frac1H\cdot\frac{H(3H+1)}{2}$, which is \eqref{eq:randommean}.
\end{proof}

A route to the Riemann hypothesis through the Liouville function claimed $E_{2}(X,H)=\sum_{H<h\le2H}C_{h}(X)^{2}\ll X^{2+\epsilon}/H$, and deduced $\sum_{n\le X}\lambda(n)\ll X^{1/2+\epsilon}$. Divided by the $H$ shifts, the claim bounds the mean square correlation by $X^{2+\epsilon}/H^{2}$, while \eqref{eq:randommean} is $X(1+o(1))$ for $H=o(X)$; the claimed bound is the smaller one as soon as $H$ exceeds $X^{1/2+\epsilon}$ by more than a constant factor. The Liouville function follows the random model closely: at $X=6000$ and $H=1053$, close to $X^{0.8}$, the exact mean square is $4187$, against $4420$ from \eqref{eq:randommean} and $32.5$ allowed by the claim with $\epsilon=0$, a factor of $129$; at $X=4\cdot10^{6}$ and $H=X^{0.8}$ the factor is $8498$.\footnote{The exact values of $E_{2}(X,H)/(XH)$ lie between $0.52$ and $1.01$ for $X=10^{5}$, $10^{6}$, $4\cdot10^{6}$ and $H=X^{0.5},X^{0.6},\dots,X^{0.9}$; the ratio falls below one at the largest $H$ because \eqref{eq:randommean} loses the term $\frac32H$. The claimed bound is too small by factors that grow like $H^{2}/X$, from $0.9$ at $H=X^{1/2}$, where the claim is consistent with the data, to $1.3\cdot10^{5}$ at $X=4\cdot10^{6}$, $H=X^{0.9}$. Cancellation of this kind is not visible shift by shift: a bound $|C_{h}(X)|\ll X^{\theta}$ for each $h$ separately is a bound on the absolute values, and the random model already gives each $C_{h}$ the size $X^{1/2}$.} The screen rejects any claimed bound on the mean square below the exact mean divided by four, the factor leaving room for small $X$; in words, a correlation bound below the random-model size $\sqrt X$ per shift is rejected.

\emph{Unit fractions.} The Erd\H{o}s--Straus conjecture says that $\frac4n=\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z$ has a solution in positive integers for every $n\ge2$. It has been checked for every $n\le10^{18}$ \cite{MB25} and is open. For a prime $p$, a solution of $\frac4p=\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z$ is of \emph{type I} when $p$ divides exactly one of $x,y,z$ and of \emph{type II} when it divides exactly two \cite{ET13}.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:egyptian}
Every solution with $x\le y\le z$ satisfies $\frac n4<x\le\frac{3n}{4}$ and, writing $\frac4n-\frac1x=\frac ab$, also $\frac ba<y\le\frac{2b}{a}$; given $x$ and $y$, the value $z$ is determined. So every $n$ has finitely many solutions, and they can be listed with exact arithmetic.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
From $\frac1x<\frac4n$ we get $x>\frac n4$, and from $\frac4n=\frac1x+\frac1y+\frac1z\le\frac3x$ we get $x\le\frac{3n}{4}$. In the same way $\frac ab=\frac1y+\frac1z$ gives $\frac1y<\frac ab$ and $\frac ab\le\frac2y$, that is $\frac ba<y\le\frac{2b}a$. Finally $\frac1z=\frac ab-\frac1y$ fixes $z$, which must be a positive integer at least $y$.
\end{proof}

For $n=73$ the lemma gives seven solutions: four of type I, among them $\frac1{20}+\frac1{210}+\frac1{30660}$ with only $30660=73\cdot420$ divisible by $73$, and three of type II. A normal form for the solutions that covers only type II therefore misses four of the seven, and the screen tests every proposed normal form against all solutions of small cases. A second screen tests every claimed existence of a parameter in the same way. One argument needed, for a prime $p$ and a prime $q\equiv3\pmod4$, a divisor $k$ of $\frac{q+1}{4}$ with $q\mid p+4k^{2}$; at $p=97$ and $q=7$ the candidates are $k=1,2$, and neither $101$ nor $113$ is a multiple of $7$. For $p=97$ no prime $q\equiv3\pmod4$ below $400$ has such a divisor.\footnote{Both screens are short programs, and the second shows why the claimed step carries the whole difficulty: the classical families of solutions cover the primes $p$ in fixed residue classes, and Mordell's observation, recalled by Elsholtz and Tao \cite{ET13}, is that no finite set of such families covers the primes in the residue classes that are squares, so a proof must produce for each $p$ a divisor in a progression, which is the open part of the conjecture. A proof that ends by choosing a suitable modulus or divisor without showing that one exists is rejected by the second screen at the first $p$ for which none does.} The package records the conjecture as an open problem whose barriers are Mordell's families and the two screens, and it does not count a verification up to any bound as a route to a proof.

\emph{Series over primes.} A constant given by a series over primes is known only as well as its tail. The asymptotic of Dahan's Type~II sum for the Erd\H{o}s--Straus equation \cite{Dah26} carries a constant given by such a series, whose terms decay like $(\log p)^{2}/p^{2}$ and not like $p^{-3}$; cut at $10^{6}$ with no tail term, its value was wrong by $1.7\cdot10^{-6}$. The screen accepts a value of $\sum_{p}f(p)$ only when the partial sum plus an estimate of the tail gives the same value, within the stated precision, at two cutoffs $N$ and $N'\ge10N$. For the prime zeta value $\sum_{p}p^{-2}=0.45224742\ldots$ the tail beyond $N$ is close to $E_{1}(\log N)$.\footnote{By the prime number theorem the primes near $t$ have density $1/\log t$, so $\sum_{p>N}p^{-2}$ is close to $\int_{N}^{\infty}\frac{dt}{t^{2}\log t}$, and the substitution $t=e^{u}$ turns this integral into $\int_{\log N}^{\infty}e^{-u}\frac{du}{u}=E_{1}(\log N)$. Since $E_{1}(x)=e^{-x}\bigl(\frac1x-\frac1{x^{2}}+O(x^{-3})\bigr)$, the tail is $\frac{1}{N\log N}\bigl(1-\frac{1}{\log N}+\cdots\bigr)$; a cutoff alone therefore leaves an error of order $1/(N\log N)$, which falls only by a factor slightly above ten for each factor ten in $N$.} With it the values at $10^{4}$ and $10^{5}$ agree to $6\cdot10^{-8}$, and the second is within $1.5\cdot10^{-9}$ of the true value, while the partial sum alone at $10^{5}$ is still $8\cdot10^{-7}$ short.

\emph{Definition repair.} In an interview in October 2026, Kontsevich told how an AI system, shown the text of a talk, proposed without being asked a better definition and how to correct the old one, and said that the right definition is the one from which powerful consequences follow \cite{Kon26}.\footnote{He named neither the talk nor the system nor the definition, and no account of the episode has been published, so it is an anecdote and not a result. What Anaxia takes from it is the criterion, which is old in mathematics; the gate below is ours.} Anaxia turns the criterion into a gate. Fix a finite universe $U$ of examples and a library $\mathcal L$ of predicates on $U$, the known consequences. A definition $D\subseteq U$ \emph{carries} $Q\in\mathcal L$ when $Q$ holds on every element of $D$. A new definition $D'$ is accepted as a \emph{repair} of $D$ when every element of the symmetric difference $D\triangle D'$ comes with a stated reason, and either $D'$ carries every consequence that $D$ carries and at least one more, or $D'=D$ with fewer clauses. On $U=\{1,\dots,199\}$, ``divisible only by $1$ and itself'' and ``exactly two divisors'' differ only at $1$; with the reason that $1$ is a unit, the second gains the consequences $n>1$ and $\varphi(n)=n-1$ and is accepted, while ``odd'' as a replacement for ``exactly two divisors'' loses Euclid's lemma and is refused. An accepted repair is a proposal and passes the usual checks before anything uses it.

\emph{The witness gate.} Bedrossian has formalized in Lean~4 the theorem of Mouhot and Villani on nonlinear Landau damping \cite{MV11,Bed26}, with most of the code written by AI agents from his lecture notes. The audit of that development records a conditional theorem whose hypotheses were all met by the zero datum, so that the implication said nothing about any real solution, and closes the gap by exhibiting a witness together with facts showing that it is not degenerate.\footnote{The formalization covers small backgrounds and Gevrey indices $\frac13<s<1$; its public results carry hypotheses beyond the source theorem, which the paper lists as divergences, and the restriction $s<1$ is listed among its shortcomings. It uses Lean v4.29.1 and declares no \texttt{sorry} and no axiom of its own; it is cited here and not built. The same audit keeps sentences that later evidence falsified, marked as withdrawn, which is the rule of Anaxia's log that a record is never deleted.} Anaxia adopts the rule as a gate: a conditional result counts only with an exhibited witness that meets every hypothesis and every listed nondegeneracy condition. Without one it is filed as conditional and never counts towards a closure or a benchmark.
