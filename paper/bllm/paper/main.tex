\begin{abstract}
\itshape
A system that improves itself keeps every change that passes a check, so a check that makes errors lets mistakes pile up. We compute the least checking that keeps such a system sound. It is an entropy of the costs of the changes, and an endless chain of improvements stays sound at finite cost exactly when that entropy is finite. A stack of checked layers costs more than its layers would cost alone. We also compute how many guesses a proposer needs before a right one appears: human authors come close to this least number, and language models need $1.5$ to $4.3$ times as many. Anaxia, an engine built on these laws, rebuilt the published 2016 counterexample to Steinberg's conjecture from the rules of a colouring problem alone. It learns from its failures, changing one of its own rules after each attempt that fails, and it proved a conjecture on the four-dimensional reflexive polytopes behind Calabi--Yau threefolds: shrunk one reflexive polytope at a time, each of them reaches a seed in at most $\seedBound$ steps. The engine is a work in progress, and its rules and search are being revised and extended for the open problems it has not yet settled.
\par\medskip\upshape\noindent\textit{2020 Mathematics Subject Classification.} 68T05; 68T20; 94A17; 60J80; 05C15; 52B20.
\end{abstract}
