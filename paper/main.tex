\begin{abstract}
\itshape
We prove that certifying self-modifications of total cost $A$ with at most $\theta$ expected false acceptances costs $(A/\log\eps^{-1})(H(p)+\log\theta^{-1})$ up to an additive $A$, that a sound infinite self-improvement of finite cost exists exactly when $\sum_{n}a_{n}\log(1/a_{n})$ converges, and that a proposer needs at least $e^{H_{1/2}(\pi)}$ proposals, a bound that language models miss by factors of $1.5$ to $4.3$. A typed crossing network with shared memory, run on five open problems including the Yang--Mills mass gap and the Hodge conjecture, reaches what its libraries can reach with half the checks, including a lattice mass gap for $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ on $\beta_{W}<\frac13$ that we prove; Hebbian boosters add nothing, and none of the five problems is solved.
\par\medskip\upshape\noindent\textit{2020 Mathematics Subject Classification.} 68T05; 68T20; 94A17; 60J80.\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{ACM Computing Classification.} Computing methodologies $\to$ Search methodologies; Theory of computation $\to$ Logic and verification.\par\smallskip\noindent\textit{Keywords.} Recursive self-improvement; verification; entropy; automated discovery.
\end{abstract}
